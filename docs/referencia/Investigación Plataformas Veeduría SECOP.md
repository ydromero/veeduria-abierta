# **Architecture and Analysis of Open Contracting Systems: Integration of SECOP II, OCDS and Algorithmic Anomaly Detection**

## **Economic Foundations and the Public Procurement Ecosystem**

The economics of public procurement represents a distinctive economic and computational problem of immense complexity, primarily because the State plays a dual role: it acts simultaneously as the massive purchaser of goods and services and as the sovereign legislator that establishes the rules of the game for the system.1 At a global level, this ecosystem handles trillions of dollars annually, becoming the main vector of interaction between public funds and the private sector.2 Academic literature and empirical analyses organize this cycle into five major sequential stages: demand planning, tender preparation, bid submission and contract award, contract execution, and long-term economic consequences.1
At the heart of this architecture lies a chain of nested agency relationships linking three independent but interconnected actors. The first actor is the central designer (the legislator or regulatory body), who establishes the legal framework, financial thresholds, and permitted resources. The second actor is the decentralized public buyer, that is, the contracting entity (which may be a ministry, a municipality, a hospital, or an autonomous corporation). This entity has the operational responsibility for transforming a public need into a tender specification, conducting the competition, and administering the resulting contract. The third actor comprises private sector companies, which act strategically and are not homogeneous. In any bidding process, the corporate universe is divided into winners, active bidders who competed unsuccessfully, subcontractors who participate through the winner, and fully capable suppliers who choose not to participate.
The dynamics between these actors do not occur in a computational or economic vacuum; they are intrinsically conditioned by the two-dimensional market environment surrounding each tender. On the supply side, the number of capable suppliers and the entry cost determine the maximum level of competition a process can attract. On the demand side, the scale of the public need and its fragmentation dictate the buyer's bargaining power. When this delicate balance is disrupted, the results are detrimental to public funds: competition diminishes, efficient suppliers choose to withdraw from the system, and projects suffer chronic delays, massive cost overruns, or, in the worst-case scenario, are never delivered.
Empirical data reveal an alarming global trend toward monopolistic consolidation and the erosion of competition in public markets. For example, consolidated metrics indicate that the average number of bidders per government contract has experienced dramatic declines in the last decade, falling from an average of 5.7 to 3.2 bidders, while single - bid awards have climbed from 24% to an alarming 42% in various jurisdictions.1 This market concentration transforms procurement mechanisms, shifting them from highly competitive, fixed-price systems toward direct negotiations and cost-plus models, paving the way for structural inefficiency and institutional corruption.1
To combat these information asymmetries, mitigate the risks of state capture, and maximize the value of taxpayer money, international organizations such as the Open Contracting Partnership (OCP) have led the digitization and algorithmic opening of procurement data.3 The premise of open contracting is that the availability of structured, end-to-end data makes it possible to prevent and combat corruption by strengthening democratic institutions, improving the delivery of government services by increasing efficiency, promoting the economic inclusion of small and medium-sized enterprises, and increasing resilience and environmental sustainability.3 Through the application of continuous algorithmic auditing, modern systems seek to proactively (ex ante) identify anomalies before contracts are signed and financial resources are irreparably disbursed.5

## **Analysis of Technological Benchmarks in the Global Market**

Building a public procurement analytics platform requires a thorough review of technological solutions already operating successfully at the international forefront. Applications such as ProZorro, OpenTender, and Spend Network have addressed complex architectural paradigms related to massive data ingestion, fraud detection using predictive algorithms, and the visual representation of complex corruption networks. Understanding what these solutions do and how they present information to the end user is imperative for establishing the baseline for the new system to be developed.

### **ProZorro's Hybrid and Decentralized Architecture**

ProZorro is an electronic public procurement system originating in Ukraine that has redefined business trust in the state through a centralized database architecture coupled with multiple decentralized interfaces.<sup>4</sup> ProZorro operates as the government's immutable core (backend), storing all tenders, bids, contracts, and appeals. However, it does not provide a single, state-monopolized graphical interface for businesses to interact with. Instead, it allows accredited commercial platforms (marketplaces operated by the private sector) to connect to the central database via a bidirectional Application Programming Interface (API).<sup>6</sup>
This architecture fosters innovation in the presentation layer and ensures an extreme level of transparency, as all trading platforms reflect the exact same database in real time. For the end user, whether an auditor or a supplier, information is presented through clear transaction flows. One of ProZorro's biggest differentiators is its electronic reverse auction engine: the platform forces bidders to lower their prices in three anonymous competitive rounds, showing the user dynamic dashboards where costs fall in real time, generating substantial immediate savings and eliminating the possibility of under-the-table price-fixing during bid opening.

### **Civic Technology and OpenTender Network Analysis**

OpenTender positions itself within the civic tech spectrum as a platform explicitly designed to promote advanced analytical visualization of public procurement.6 Unlike traditional government portals that limit the user to simple search bars, OpenTender extracts and processes millions of fragmented data points to build interactive dashboards that assess market integrity at both macro and micro levels.6
The way OpenTender presents data to the user breaks with the reliance on spreadsheets. It uses network graph-based visualization paradigms, where purchasing entities and supplier companies are represented as nodes, and contracts act as the edges or links connecting them. This approach allows journalists, researchers, and citizens to visually detect de facto monopolies or collusion rings: clusters where a small group of companies systematically wins all the contracts from a specific municipality. Furthermore, the platform calculates and illustrates "integrity scores" for each tender based on a predefined set of risk indicators, translating complex mathematical calculations into thermal color scales easily interpreted by non-technical users.

### **Predictive Analytics and Global Aggregation with Spend Network**

Spend Network represents the predictive evolution of open contracting analysis, using data science and machine learning techniques for global aggregation and prediction of systemic failures4. What Spend Network does is ingest transactional databases from multiple world governments and cross-reference this information with corporate beneficial ownership records to track who the true owners of capital behind shell companies are4.
From the user's perspective, Spend Network displays risk-oriented business intelligence dashboards. Instead of simply conducting forensic audits of past contracts, the platform projects future risk. It presents users with statistical predictions, alerting them, for example, that a recently published tender has an 80 percent chance of failing due to a lack of bidders, or that it is designed in such a way that it will likely result in massive cost overruns. This shift from descriptive analytics (what happened) to predictive analytics (what will happen) is the paradigm that maximizes the use of information in the public sector.

## **The Source of Operational Truth: SECOP II and the SODA API Architecture**

To build a platform with these capabilities within the Colombian jurisdictional context, the essential source of truth is the Electronic Public Procurement System (SECOP II) managed by Colombia Compra Eficiente. SECOP II is a purely transactional platform that connects public entities and suppliers to carry out official online procurement processes, enabling document management from planning to contract settlement.7
All the immense wealth generated in SECOP II, in order to support traceability and transparency, is consolidated and published on the government open data portal (datos.gov.co) using the SODA API (Socrata Open Data API) infrastructure.9 The most critical dataset for the project has the identifier jbjy-vk9h and contains the granular record of all electronic contracts entered into on the platform, currently exceeding 5.6 million rows of continuous historical information7.

### **Ingestion Mechanics and SODA Communication Protocols**

The design of the synchronization module for interacting with the SODA API must adhere to high-efficiency architectural principles to avoid exhausting local server memory or overloading government nodes. All communication is encrypted using the HTTPS protocol, and operational errors are strictly reported using standard HTTP9 response codes.
The SODA API supports content negotiation via HTTP Accept headers, enabling data transfer in multiple structural formats, including JSON, XML, CSV, and even GeoJSON for direct geographic mapping applications.9 In the context of modern application development (such as a backend built on Laravel and PostgreSQL), the JSON format is imperative due to its low network weight and the native ease of current frameworks in deserializing its strings into manageable objects.
To ensure stability and compliance with quotas for mass data extraction (throttling), the use of an Application Token (App Token) issued by Socrata is absolutely mandatory. This cryptographic identifier is injected into each request using the HTTP header X-App-Token or by appending it to the end of the URL string with the parameter app_token9 . Operating without this token will result in the system being blocked by government anti-DDoS security policies.
Furthermore, the volume of 5.6 million records prohibits full bulk read operations in a single transaction. The system must implement comprehensive pagination routines using Socrata Query Language (SoQL), utilizing the offset ($ offset) and limit ($limit) parameters. SoQL allows complex filtering to be delegated to the Socrata server, making it possible, for example, to request only those contracts that have changed their status in the last 24 hours, or to filter using stacking parameters on text strings.

## **The Open Contracting Data Standard (OCDS) as a Universal Scheme**

While extracting data from SECOP II provides the fuel for analysis,11 basing the analytical database structure solely on SODA column names results in a fragile system highly dependent on the idiosyncrasies of local bureaucracy. For information to truly become "Actionable Intelligence," and to utilize global analytical repositories, raw data must be mapped, transformed, and persisted under the Open Contracting Data Standard (OCDS) promoted by the Open Contracting Partnership.8
The OCDS defines a common and comprehensive JSON data model specifically structured to reveal documents and tabular information through each and every stage of the public procurement process.12 Its central purpose is to address the historical disconnect between financial budget databases, transactional tender portals, and on-the-ground results audit systems.13
The time flow modeled by OCDS encompasses five fundamental sections (arrays within the JSON document): Planning, which links goals and budgets; Tendering, which describes the selection method and time windows; Awards, which documents the winning bidders; Contracts; and finally, Implementation, which tracks financial milestones, actual payments, and structural delays.

### **Storage Paradigms: Releases versus Records**

The most complex architectural challenge in tracking a contract over the years is the mutability of the data: budgets change, deadlines are extended, and supplier companies may be transferred. The OCDS standard elegantly addresses this through a bimodal data architecture based on "Releases" and "Records"14.
A Release is an absolutely immutable JSON document that represents a specific update in the procurement process timeline.14 For example, when a tender is initially published, a Release is generated and stored . Weeks later, when the tender specifications are modified to extend the closing date, a new Release is issued , without ever altering or overwriting the original document. This chain of immutable events is what guarantees the forensic trail for legal audits, allowing for the reconstruction of exactly what the entity knew and on what precise date it knew it.14
However, for an end user viewing a dashboard, dynamically processing dozens of immutable Releases to derive the current state would require immense computing power in the browser. To address this, OCDS provides Records. A Record is a compiled snapshot representing the consolidated and definitive state of a contracting process at the present time. The application logic on the server must handle updating the Record atomically each time it ingests a new Release . In terms of relational database design, the application will display Records for quick read operations in the user interface, but will use the Release history tables to feed the AI models and timeline reconstruction.

### **Classification of Depth Levels and Code Lists (Codelists)**

The OCDS categorizes the data that institutions must supply into three levels of adoption: Basic (which provides a general outline of contracting), Intermediate (which requires linking data between different internal State systems to track characteristics over time), and Advanced (which demands granular information on precise geographic location and third-party subcontracting schemes)13.
To achieve data universality, the architecture relies heavily on Codelists. Closed lists are immutable enumerations of values (e.g., predefined adjudication types); if a field is associated with a closed list, the database must strictly reject any external values to maintain international compatibility. Open lists, on the other hand, recommend a set of standardized values but provide flexibility to accept local legal designations (such as the unique nomenclatures of the Colombian State).14 This allows the global schema to be adapted to local needs without corrupting the underlying mathematical model.

## **Data Exploitation: The Red Flags Computational Engine **

With the information consolidated from the SODA API and impeccably modeled under the JSON architecture of the OCDS standard, the system is positioned to execute its highest value-added function: the algorithmic engine for anomaly detection and fraud prevention.
The so-called "Red Flags" are mathematical and logical heuristics based on the forensic principle that corruption, collusion, and administrative fraud leave predictable, calculable, and repetitive traces within the metadata of public contracts.5 A red flag, by itself, is not judicial proof of a crime; it is a direct or indirect indicator of potential risk that allows prioritizing the immense volumes of data towards those files that require immediate human intervention.5
To ensure that this engine does not become an indecipherable black box and that it maintains ease of technical maintenance, the methodologies implemented by agencies such as the National Directorate of Public Procurement (Paraguay) and Colombia Efficient Procurement establish that each red flag must be formally documented as a structured query (SQL Query) and possess a strict semantics17. Each anomaly is coded using seven parametric axes: an identification number, a functional description, an implementation indicator, the identification method (expert rules, probability or Machine Learning), the simplified calculation formula, the mathematical technique used (e.g. quartile analysis, threshold exceedance), and the logical adjustment parameters17.
The computational logic for implementing six of the most critical red flags in the global ecosystem, mapped directly to the nodes of the OCDS scheme, is detailed below, based on documentation from open-source tools such as Cardinal4:

### **Flag 1: Tender with a Single Bidder ( Single Bidder)* *

As discussed in the economic rationale, the absence of competition is the clearest symptom of a captured market, tailor-made specifications (designed to favor a third party), or intentionally induced disinterest. This anomaly monitors processes that, by law, should be highly competitive, but which culminate in only one company making a proposal.

* **OCDS Route :* * tender/id to group the process and awards/suppliers/id to count the suppliers12.
* **Algorithmic Implementation :* * The processing engine groups the database by each tender identifier. It then iterates over the suppliers, assigning a progressive mathematical sequence (seq=1, seq=2, etc.) to each distinct bidder. At the end of the iteration, the algorithm performs a conditional validation: if the process was marked as competitive, but the maximum value of the supplier sequence for that tender/id is strictly equal to 1, a true binary variable (1) is inserted, triggering the alert. Methodological care must be taken not to trigger this flag in hyper-specialized niche markets where the existence of a natural monopoly is documented and justified.12

### **Flag 2: Unreasonably Short Bidding Timeframes**

Providing a minuscule timeframe between the publication of the tender documents and the deadline for submitting complex proposals is a classic mechanism of procedural fraud. This tactic grants an insurmountable unfair advantage to a pre-selected contractor, who has been tipped off and has secretly prepared their extensive documentation before the official announcement.<sup>12</sup>

* ** OCDS Route :* * Time nodes tender/tenderPeriod/startDate and tender/tenderPeriod/endDate12.
* **Algorithmic Implementation :* * The system extracts both timestamps and performs a time subtraction operation in the backend. If the absolute value of the difference, measured in calendar or business days according to the rule's parameterization, is less than a suspicion threshold (for example, less than or equal to three days depending on the regime or amount of the contract), the system injects the binary flag (1), alerting the competition to an artificial time barrier.

### **Flag 3: Victory of a Supplier with No Prior History ( Front Company)* *

The creation of ephemeral companies (shell companies) just before large contracts is a standard vehicle for capital diversion, concealment of real beneficiaries and money laundering4.

* **OCDS Path :* * References to awards/suppliers/id, dates tender/tenderPeriod/endDate and status awards/status12.
* **Algorithmic Implementation :* * The algorithm processes the request by comparing the tender date against the universal historical table of suppliers. If, when performing a count of the specific supplier's previous participations, the result is zero immediately before the closing date, and simultaneously the tender condition evaluates that this inexperienced supplier obtained the absolute victory (awards/ winning status ), an alarm is triggered. The literature warns (citing experts such as Mihály Fazekas) that this flag should be analyzed in conjunction with others, since the entry of a legitimate new competitor could, paradoxically, be a symptom of a healthy market opening up to innovation.12

### **Flag 4: Offer Excessively Close to the Estimated Budget**

The millimeter convergence (for example, with differences of just cents or 0.01%) between the amount that the entity reserved in secret and the economic offer delivered in a sealed envelope by the winning proponent, almost invariably demonstrates the leakage of internal confidential information12.

* **Algorithmic Implementation :* * The percentage deviation between the value structured in planning/budget/amount/value (the ceiling budget) and the final evaluated economic proposal is assessed. When the variance between both amounts is less than a minimum analytical tolerance percentile configured in the business rule, the engine classifies the scenario as a likely pre-bid corrupt agreement.

### **Flag 5: Offers Separated by an Exact Mathematical Percentage**

When multiple bidders participate in a tender, but numerical analysis reveals that bidder A's proposal is consistently 2.5% cheaper than bidder B's, and bidder B's proposal is exactly 2.5% cheaper than bidder C's, this pattern is statistically improbable in a free market. This indicates horizontal collusive behavior, where bidders appear to compete but actually operate as a cartel using simple price-scaling algorithms to secure distributed market shares.<sup>12</sup>

* **Algorithmic Implementation :* * The computational engine processes all bids received under the same tender/id. A matrix of percentage deltas is calculated between each of the economic proposals. If the variance of these deltas asymptotically approaches zero, revealing an artificially equidistant spacing between bids, the collusion heuristic is activated.

### **Flag 6: Contract Splitting * *

Contract splitting is the perverse practice of dividing a large structural project (such as the construction of a hospital) into multiple small, repetitive contracts. The sole purpose of this division is to keep each individual contract just below the legal monetary threshold that would require the entity to hold an open public tender, thus allowing them to award contracts directly through repeated contracting.

* **Algorithmic Implementation :* * This is one of the most computationally demanding algorithms. It requires evaluating temporary groupings of parties. The engine sums the total monetary value of awards directly granted by the same public entity to the same consortium or company, within the same UNSPSC code category, in a predefined time window (for example, a fiscal semester). If the aggregate sum of these smaller contracts aggressively exceeds the legal threshold for public bidding established in the jurisdiction, the system infers and alerts about deliberate systematic contract splitting.

## **Predictive Modeling and Machine Learning: The Analytical Frontier (The VIGIA Case)* *

The expert rules documented above provide a formidable foundation for reactive detection. However, the platform's full potential is realized when the system transitions from deterministic thresholds to stochastic machine learning models capable of processing the enormous dimensionality of the data.
An example applied to Colombian contracting is the development of institutional risk models and indices such as VIGIA, which are designed to prioritize contract supervision using neural networks and classification algorithms7.
Data preparation (data munging) for these algorithms involves massive transformations. According to applied academic research on SECOP II, 156,520 procurement processes and a refined set of 87,387 contracts focused on specific regions (such as territorial entities of Bogotá) were consolidated, where the completion date guaranteed that the physical works should have been finished.7 To clean the ingestion, duplicate records are reconciled by selecting, for example, the oldest publication date in case of conflicts.7
A revolutionary element in this modeling is the ingestion of data from third parties unrelated to the transactional platform. The quality of the predictive model increases substantially when historical records of 14,150 sanctions imposed on legal entities and individuals by regulatory bodies (such as the Superintendency of Industry and Commerce) between 2015 and 2020 are incorporated, related to cases of corporate cartelization, unfair competition, or serious deficiencies in consumer service.7
Using this enriched data and the semantic groupings provided by the UNSPSC universal codes, the predictor variable matrix is generated. The target variable, or outcome variable, that the algorithm must learn to predict is derived from the formal additions and modifications to the contract (stored in the SECOP II—Additions records). If the historical document reports post-signing financial overruns, substantial modifications, or massive delays in the delivery date, the algorithm internally labels it with the numerical vector (1). If the contract was completed flawlessly within the original budget, it is assigned the vector (0). After supervised training, the model acquires the mathematical capacity to read the characteristics of a newly published contract and issue an exact percentage probability that the project will be plagued by corruption or cost overruns, surgically guiding the limited efforts of the fiscal control and oversight apparatus.

## **Implementation Strategy for the Development Environment (Laravel, Livewire 3 and PostgreSQL)* *

The practical viability of this massive theoretical framework rests entirely on the engineering team's architectural capabilities to implement it within the designated technical ecosystem. Given that this analysis will serve as the primary reference document for the internally developed artificial intelligence module (PI) in a project with similar technical characteristics to the Sonnar GPON module, it is essential to outline extremely rigid implementation guidelines anchored in PHP (Laravel), Livewire 3, and PostgreSQL 18.

### **Backend Architecture, Atomic Ingestion, and Relational Integrity**

The transfer of millions of records from the government portal datos.gov.co via the SODA API places a heavy load on the infrastructure. The ingestion processes must be built using asynchronous background routines (Laravel Background Jobs), managed through robust queues (Redis/Horizon) to iteratively process the SoQL9 pagination batches ($limit and $offset). These processes must always be launched through the standardized container (by convention, using vendor/bin/sail)18.
To prevent corruption of the local database in the event of network outages or API failures, every write operation that implements OCDS Releases and Records must be strictly and unconditionally wrapped within atomic blocks provided by the framework ( DB:: transaction). This ensures that if an insert fails midway through building a contract's history, the entire block is preemptively discarded, keeping the database free of orphaned nodes or destroyed logical entities.<sup>18</sup>
Furthermore, business integrity rules require that artificial or false null values not be inserted into columns configured by the PostgreSQL engine as NOT NULL simply to bypass temporary validations. If SODA fails to provide vital information during ingestion, the Laravel ingestion system must treat this as a critical error, log it in the exception log, and report it to the administrator.18 Logical validation and parameterization (dictionary links that do not use direct foreign keys in the database) demand impeccable design in Eloquent Models to avoid breaking predictive queries for red flags.

### **User Interface (UI) Design and Reactive State Management**

Presenting the findings to users—how the data is displayed in a usable way—requires moving away from visual data dumps. Replicating successful civic technology paradigms like OpenTender, the application should use the structural frameworks of Bootstrap 4 and Bootstrap Icons to natively build the interface, minimizing the burden of additional design libraries.<sup>18</sup>
The primary view of the dashboards should be informed by mathematical aggregations of red flags. These visuals will be built using "KPI Cards" (Key Performance Indicators) at the top of the interface, displaying dynamic counters that summarize the health of the institutional network: financial value trapped in horizontal collusion alerts, percentage of contracts executed directly versus through bidding, and supplier topologies with the highest accumulated risk flags.
At a microscopic level, the data tables for exploring individual contracts, built with isolated components of Livewire 3, must operate using strict database-controlled pagination techniques ( paginate( ) in Laravel), in full compliance with the read economy policy.18 Attempting to load the entire context of all historical contracts into Livewire properties is computationally catastrophic. When a user requests to thoroughly explore a contract flagged with red flags, a contextual interactive modals or Show Views approach should be used to asynchronously retrieve and display the complete OCDS lineage (its exact publication dates, financial award, and vendor links) only when the user triggers the specific action, thus isolating the reactive state from the rest of the main interface.21

### **Automated Auditing and Testing**

The algorithmic ecosystem demands impeccable quality assurance (QA). During the development of the Red Flags engine and the orchestration of bulk ingestion using controllers and services (Services/Actions), any functional testing involving database interaction must completely avoid destructive tools for global temporary cleanup. In compliance with the most rigorous development standards for this type of project (and the rules stipulated for the PI agent), validations must rely on the DatabaseTransactions trait provided by PHPUnit/Pest18. This ensures that after each algorithmic assertion evaluating whether a bid was correctly flagged as anomalous, all data inserted during the test is gracefully undone, achieving agile, independent code coverage that is absolutely free of residual contamination in the persistent datasets.
In conclusion, the implementation of a modern public procurement scrutiny system through the integration of SECOP II, the SODA API, OCDS heuristics, and predictive models like VIGIA requires the development of a sophisticated hybrid architecture. The transformative power to uncover cartels and optimize public funds no longer lies in data collection, but rather in the application of rigorous mathematical methodology to a resilient software ecosystem capable of seamlessly processing the vast historical dimension of the interaction between the state and the market.
